# Research prompt: design the ideal `twin_profile.md` for a local digital twin

Paste everything inside the fenced block below into claude.ai with Research turned on. The output has five deliverables; D1 becomes the ground rules in `docs/PLAN2.md`, D2 becomes the parser spec for `twin/profile.py`, D3 replaces the interview part of `docs/opus_profile_prompt.md`, D4 feeds the item-bank evaluation, and D5 goes into `data/twin_profile.example.md` to test the parser.

````
I am building a local "digital twin": a small language model system that answers questions as me, in my voice, and predicts my decisions and survey answers. I want you to research how to design the ONE markdown profile file the twin reads, so that the twin is as faithful as possible under my hardware limits, and so that I can evaluate it the way Stanford's generative-agent work evaluates its agents. Research first, then produce the deliverables in the exact structure at the end.

## My setup (fixed constraints, do not propose changing them)

- Everything runs on one Windows laptop with an 8 GB GPU. Models: an 8B chat model for the twin's voice (Llama-3 based, 8192-token context), qwen3-8b at 8192 context for structured JSON decisions and forced-choice answers, hermes3 8B for tool calls, llama3.1 8B and qwen2.5 7B as LLM judges, nomic-embed-text and embeddinggemma (768-dim) for retrieval, and qwen3:8b at 40960 context for one-shot summaries only. No cloud model in the loop except an optional Claude judge in evaluation.
- The twin reads exactly one markdown file, data/twin_profile.md. Software splits it into retrieval chunks: every "# Section" is a section and every "## Subsection" is one chunk; a section without subsections is one chunk. Chunks are embedded and the top 5 are placed in the prompt per question, after a static prefix (Identity, Voice rules, 3 voice samples, Boundaries, and a digest of at most 400 tokens generated once from the whole file). So the whole file is never in context; only the prefix plus 5 chunks, inside 8192 tokens.
- The parser depends on these labels, which must stay parseable (you may add new sections and subsections, but do not rename these): "# Identity"; "# Voice" with "## Style rules" and "## Sample 1" ... "## Sample 15" (verbatim messages I wrote); "# Values"; "# Preferences" with "## Food", "## Tech and tools", "## Work style", "## Free time", "## Money", "## Communication"; "# People" (roles only, no real names); "# Decisions" with "## D-01: title" blocks containing "Situation:", "Options: A | B [| C]", "Choice:", "Why:", "Outcome:" lines (15 to 20 of them); "# Goals"; "# Boundaries"; "# Eval" with "## Q-01" ... "## Q-20" blocks containing "Question:" and "Answer:" lines (gold answers in my own words; this section is excluded from retrieval). Frontmatter: name and updated date.
- Current evaluation: the twin answers the 20 Eval questions; llama3.1 and qwen2.5 grade factual agreement, voice fidelity, roleplay artifacts, and overall on 1-5; a retrieval bake-off measures recall@k and MRR across three embedders. There is no ceiling or baseline yet.
- Planned evaluation, modeled on Park et al.: a self-answered item bank (Big Five Inventory 44, a subset of General Social Survey core items, the dictator, trust, public goods and prisoner's dilemma games, plus my 20 gold answers), the twin answering the same items by JSON, accuracy for categorical items and mean absolute error plus correlation for continuous ones, normalized by my own test-retest agreement two weeks apart, compared across three conditions: demographic-only (Identity), persona (Identity plus digest), and interview (full retrieval plus expert reflections generated from an interview transcript).
- The profile will be produced from a long semi-structured interview conducted by Claude (about two hours, adaptive follow-ups), with the transcript saved and chunked as a second retrieval source. Voice samples come from real messages I paste in, not from the interview.
- Privacy rules: no passwords, addresses, account or ID numbers, no other people's real names (roles only), health details only if I explicitly approve, and the twin must deflect a list of boundary topics.

## What to research

1. Park et al., "Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People" (arXiv 2411.10109, Nov 2024) and the Stanford HAI policy brief "Simulating Human Behavior with AI Agents" (May 2025): what the interview protocol was (American Voices Project), how transcripts were used in the prompt, what the "expert reflection" step is and whether it helped, which evaluation instruments and normalization they used, how demographic and persona baselines were built, and every reported number I should benchmark against.
2. Follow-up and related work (2024-2026) on interview-based or transcript-based persona agents: which parts of an interview carry the most predictive signal (life history, values, routines, decisions, self-descriptions), whether summaries lose accuracy versus verbatim text, and any evidence about retrieval-augmented persona agents when the full transcript does not fit the context window.
3. Freely usable instruments: the exact licensing and source for the BFI-44 (John and Srivastava) or a free alternative such as the BFI-2 or IPIP items; which GSS core items are public and suitable; standard parametrizations of the four economic games; recommended item counts for a single-person test-retest design; how to compute normalized accuracy and what reliability to expect from a two-week retest.
4. Semi-structured interview design: block structure, time budgets, seed questions, follow-up strategies ("tell me about a time when", probing for specifics), what to capture verbatim, and how to elicit decisions with situation, options, reasoning and outcome.
5. Style imitation from few-shot samples: evidence on how many verbatim samples matter, what style rules an LLM can actually follow at 8B scale, and how to describe punctuation, slang, and register so a small model reproduces them.
6. Privacy and redaction practices for interview-derived agents: what to strip, consent and deletion norms the paper proposes, and how to write boundary rules a small model will obey.

## Deliverables (produce all five, in this order, each in its own markdown code block so I can save them as files)

D1. GROUND RULES: a numbered list of 15 to 25 rules for setting up profile.md, each with one sentence of rationale and a citation to what you found. Cover: what must be verbatim versus synthesized, target length per section in tokens given 5 chunks of context, chunk size (each "##" chunk should be self-contained and roughly 80 to 300 tokens), how to phrase facts so they retrieve well, how many decisions and eval items, what belongs in the static prefix versus retrievable chunks, ordering, what to never include, and how to keep the file stable between versions so evaluation results stay comparable.

D2. SCHEMA v2: the complete markdown skeleton of the recommended twin_profile.md, keeping every label listed above parseable and adding whatever new sections the research supports (for example interview highlights, beliefs and attitudes, routines, self-ratings, life events). For every section and subsection give: purpose, target size, verbatim or synthesized, how it is filled (interview block, pasted messages, self-report), and an example line. State explicitly which sections should be excluded from retrieval like Eval is.

D3. INTERVIEW PROTOCOL: the block list with minutes per block, 3 to 6 seed questions per block, follow-up rules, and for each block which schema sections it fills. Include the instructions the AI interviewer needs (one question at a time, push for specifics, keep my wording, skip answered topics, handle refusals).

D4. ITEM BANK AND SCORING: which instruments to use with their sources and license status, how many items from each, the response format for each item type so an 8B model can answer via a JSON schema (enum for categorical, integer range for Likert, number for game allocations), the scoring formulas, the normalization by test-retest, the three conditions to compare, and realistic expected numbers for an 8B retrieval-based twin versus the paper's frontier-model results. Do not paste copyrighted item text; link to the sources and give the item ids or a description.

D5. SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE: a complete example profile in the v2 schema for a fictional person (not me), with 15 decisions, 20 eval questions, 15 voice samples in a consistent texting voice, and every new section filled, so I can test my parser. If length forces a cut, keep the structure complete and shorten the prose, and say what was shortened.

Rules for your answer: cite sources for every factual claim about the paper, the instruments, or evaluation numbers; mark anything you could not verify as uncertain rather than guessing; do not ask me questions before delivering (make reasonable assumptions and list them at the top); keep the deliverables in the order and code-block format above; nothing about me is needed, the example is fictional.
````
