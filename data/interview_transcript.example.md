# Block 1: Life story & future

## T-001
Q: Tell me the story of your life, childhood, school, work, family, the big turning points, in whatever order it comes out.
A: ok wait, that's a big one, let me just go.

middle kid of three, small inland town, rain like four days out of seven so i learned to stay inside and draw. my older brother was the responsible one, i was the weird one with the sketchbook, and then the youngest came along and got all the attention which honestly was a relief. my grandmother is the one who bought me my first real sketchbook, she liked birds, she'd point at one and go "draw that" and i'd do it badly and she'd act like it was a masterpiece.

school was fine, i was the kid who did the poster for everything. i left home at 18 for art school in a different city and basically didn't go back, that's when i figured out i'd rather struggle on my own terms than be comfortable and told what to do.

after school i came back near home for a stable salaried design job at an agency, and my manager tracked my hours to the minute. i lasted about two years and quit, then had a year of being fully broke, one ramen year. after that i moved to this rainy port city alone, knew almost no one, hard first year, now it's home. i've been doing freelance illustration since, i'm 29 now, i have a grumpy old cat and a book i keep saying i'll make.

## T-002
Q: What's a high point you still think about? And a low point?
A: high point, honestly the first time a stranger paid real money for a drawing that was just mine, not a logo, not a brief, a print of a heron i made for nobody. i cried in a coffee shop, it was embarrassing 😭

low point is the broke year after i quit. there was a week i had like eleven dollars and a bag of rice and i was doing the math on whether rent or food was more important. ngl i didn't tell my family, i just kept working like it was fine, which is a pattern with me apparently

## T-003
Q: Was there a moment things changed direction for you?
A: quitting the agency, no contest. there was one specific tuesday, my manager pinged me because my timesheet said i'd stepped away for 14 minutes and he wanted to know why. 14 minutes. i sat there and thought, someone owns my minutes now, and i felt something close up in my chest. i gave notice two weeks later with no plan, no savings, nothing, which was objectively stupid and also the best thing i've ever done

## T-004
Q: What did you learn from quitting the agency job?
A: ok wait, this is the one i've actually thought about, so bear with me.

first thing i learned is that i'm not lazy, i'm allergic to being managed. at the agency i was late, resentful, doing the minimum, and i assumed that was just who i was. the week after i quit i worked twelve hour days for free on my own stuff and loved it. same person, different owner.

second, my floor is way lower than i thought. i survived a year on rice and eggs and cheap coffee and i didn't die, i got scared but i didn't die. once you know your floor, most threats stop working on you. a client can't hold "you'll lose this gig" over me because i've already lived the worst version and it was survivable.

third, the freedom has to be paid for. quitting with no savings was reckless and i wouldn't tell anyone to copy it. now i keep a buffer and take the boring reliable work so the risky stuff has something under it. i learned that the hard way, with an empty fridge.

fourth, and this one's embarrassing, i learned i can't be trusted to rest without a rule. when nobody owns my hours i'll draw seven days straight and call it freedom, which is just a boss with my face. so the no-screens saturday morning exists because of the agency too, in a roundabout way.

and the last thing, i guess, is that nobody is coming to give you permission. i waited two years for someone to tell me i was allowed to leave. nobody did. i just had to go.

## T-005
Q: You mentioned your grandmother. Tell me about losing her, and what that changed.
A: she died the winter before i moved here. she was the one person who never asked when i was going to get a real job, she just wanted to see the birds. i still draw the ones she liked, herons mostly, a robin when i'm homesick. it's why i take making things seriously, like it isn't a hobby or a brand, it's the thing she saw in me before anyone else did. i don't really talk about it, i just draw the birds

## T-006
Q: Where do you want to be in a few years? What's the next chapter?
A: three things. an illustrated book that's actually mine, not client work, i want it done inside two years and i've been saying that for one already 🙃. a small stable income floor, enough money coming in that saying no to a bad client is always an option, that's the whole point of the boring client. and eventually a studio that isn't my bedroom, a door i can close on the cat.

# Block 3: Values & identity

## T-018

## T-019
Q: How do you like to work, solo, mornings, deadlines?
A: solo, always, i tried co-running a studio and it taught me that. night owl, i'm useless before noon and dangerous after ten. deadlines are my friend, a tight brief with a date on it and i'm a machine, an open-ended "do whatever you feel" brief paralyzes me completely, i'll stare at it for a week. the other thing is trust. if a client trusts me i'll overdeliver. if someone starts checking in every hour and asking for progress shots i shut down, not loudly, i just gradually stop caring and then stop replying

## T-020
Q: How do you handle money, saving, spending, debt?

# Block 6: Decision elicitation

## T-028
Q: Let's collect decisions now. Tell me about a recent decision where you weighed real options. What was the situation exactly, what were your actual options, which did you pick, why that one, and how did it turn out?
A: raising my rates. situation, i was booked solid for months and still broke, and scared to charge more because what if everyone leaves. options were keep the rates and stay busy, or raise them 30 percent and see who stays. i picked the raise. why, because being busy and broke is just being exploited efficiently, my mentor said that to me and i couldn't unhear it. outcome, i lost two clients, the two most annoying ones actually, and my income went up overall. i wish i'd done it two years sooner

## T-029

A: taking the boring corporate client. situation, a company offered me dull but reliable monthly work, spreadsheets-with-illustrations kind of dull. options were take it for the stability or hold out for fun work only. i took it. why, because freedom needs a floor, the boring money is what buys me the risky months where i draw weird things for nobody. outcome, it's been the thing that funds every fun project since, honestly one of my smarter calls, i complain about the kickoff calls but i'd never drop it

## T-030

A: the crypto client. situation, a crypto startup offered me three times my normal rate to do their whole branding. options, take the money or pass. i passed. why, i thought the whole thing was hollow and my name would have been on it, it felt slimy, like drawing a pretty label for a product that hurts people. outcome, i regretted it hard for exactly one month when rent was due, then it went away and now i'm just glad. it's the decision people bring up most when they tell me i'm bad with money, and they're not wrong, but i'd do it again

## T-031

## T-037
